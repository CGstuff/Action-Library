Review

Status, frame notes and drawings — for giving and receiving feedback on animation without leaving the library.

Status

Every animation can carry a lifecycle status, shown as a coloured badge on its card and in the metadata panel:

StatusMeans
NoneNo status. The default — fine if you're working alone.
WIPIn progress.
In ReviewWaiting on feedback.
ApprovedSigned off.
Needs WorkReviewed, changes wanted.
FinalDone.

Click the status badge in the metadata panel and pick from the menu. The card updates immediately, and everyone pointed at the same library sees it.

If Action Library is in Pipeline mode, status is owned by Pipeline Control and changes made here won't stick. See modes.

Review notes

Notes are attached to a specific frame of a specific version. That's the point: "the hand pops" is much more useful pinned to frame 34 of v002 than as a sentence in a chat window.

Leaving a note

Open an animation's history (right-click › View Lineage), pick a version, scrub to the frame, and add your note. It's stamped with your name — the one you set on first run — so it's clear who said what.

Reading notes

The notes panel lists every note on that version. Each one has a button that jumps the preview straight to its frame, so you can go through feedback point by point without hunting.

Notes can be marked resolved as you deal with them, and a resolved note can be brought back if it turns out it wasn't. Deleting a note is a soft delete — it can be restored.

Notes stay with their version

Capture a new version and the old version keeps its notes. So feedback never drifts onto a take it wasn't about, and you can look back at what was said about v001 long after you're on v004.

Because notes are per version and comparison is side by side, the review loop is: note on v002 → new capture → compare v002 with v003 with both sets of notes visible. You can see whether the note was actually addressed.

Drawing on frames

Sometimes a sentence isn't enough. In the history dialog, switch on Annotate and draw directly over the preview — arrows, circles, whatever makes the point. Drawings are stored per frame, per version, and shown to whoever opens that version next.

The tools cover freehand, lines, arrows, rectangles and circles, with colour, brush size, undo, and an onion-skin/ghost option so you can see your drawing against neighbouring frames.

Drawings are attributed by colour and name, so on a shared library it's clear whose scribble is whose.

Comparing

Select two versions in the history and press Compare. Both previews play side by side and in sync, each with its own notes and drawings. This is the dailies view — the fastest way to answer "is this better?"

Sending feedback to someone else

The history dialog can export with annotations: it burns the drawings into the preview video and writes an MP4. That's the version you send to someone who doesn't have Action Library — a client, or a director who wants it in their inbox rather than in a tool.

Burning in annotations needs FFmpeg available on your system. If the export fails, that's the first thing to check.

A review workflow that works

  1. Animator captures a take and sets it to In Review.
  2. Reviewer opens the history, scrubs, leaves notes on the frames that need them, draws where words won't do, and sets Needs Work.
  3. Animator works through the notes, marking each resolved, then captures a new version.
  4. Reviewer compares the two versions, sees what changed, and sets Approved.

Everything stays attached to the animation, so in six months the history of why it looks like that is still there.
