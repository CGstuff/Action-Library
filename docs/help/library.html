The Library

Finding things again is the whole job. This page covers the folder tree, search, filters, sorting, and the two ways of viewing your cards.

The folder tree

The left panel is your way around. The top part is fixed and built for you; the middle is yours; the bottom is what you've thrown away.

FolderShows
HomeEverything in the library.
ActionsMulti-frame animations only.
PosesSingle-frame poses only.
RecentWhat you've looked at lately — see below.
FavoritesAnything you've starred.
Your foldersThe ones you create.
Archive / TrashSet aside or deleted. Only in Studio mode — see Archive & delete.

Recent is yours alone

"Recent" is tracked per machine, not in the library. If you share a library with other people, their browsing never shows up in your Recent — and yours never shows up in theirs. Your desktop and your laptop also keep separate lists, because "what I was just looking at" is a fact about a machine, not about the library.

Your folders

Make one with the + button in the header, or right-click in the tree. Drag a card onto a folder to put it there. Drag folders onto each other to nest them. Right-click a folder to rename or delete it, or to give it an icon.

An animation can be in several folders at once — a run is Locomotion and GameDev and Show01 all at the same time. Dragging a card into a folder adds it there and leaves it wherever else it already is, and it moves nothing on disk. To take it out of one folder, use Remove from folder on its right-click menu. See Organizing.

Recursive search

The tree has a recursive toggle. On (the default), selecting a folder also shows what's in its subfolders. Off, you see only that folder's own animations.

Search

The search box filters as you type, across the animation's name, description, tags and rig type. So a search for run finds a run_cycle, anything tagged running, and anything you described as "runs then stops".

That's the argument for filling in the description. Search only finds what you wrote down.

Filters

Rig type

Every animation records the rig it was made for. The rig-type dropdown narrows the grid to one of them — useful when your library holds work for several characters and only some of it will fit the rig in front of you.

Tags

The tags dropdown narrows to animations carrying a given tag. It selects one tag at a time.

Combining

Filters stack: a folder, plus a rig type, plus a tag, plus a search, all apply together. If the grid looks emptier than expected, something is probably still set — the status bar tells you what's being shown, and clicking Home clears the folder side of it.

Sorting

The sort dropdown offers Name, Date, Duration and Recent, each ascending or descending. Sorting is a display preference and survives switching folders. "Recent" sorts by your own per-machine history, same as the Recent folder.

Views

Grid and list

The toggle switches between thumbnail cards and compact rows. Grid is for recognising an animation by eye; list is for scanning names when you have a lot of them.

Card size

The slider next to the toggle scales the cards.

Card size isn't remembered between sessions yet — it goes back to its default each time you launch.

Badges

Cards carry small markers: whether it's an action or a pose, a star if it's a favorite, its review status if it has one, and a note marker if someone has left review notes on it.

Selecting and previewing

Click to select. Ctrl+click adds to the selection, Shift+click selects a range. With something selected, the right panel shows its details and plays its preview video — with play/pause, looping, speed and scrubbing.

Right-click a card for its menu: apply it, view its version history, favorite it, or remove it.

Refreshing

Action Library notices new captures on its own — it watches the library folder and checks for messages from Blender every few seconds. If something hasn't appeared, the refresh button in the header re-scans the library and rebuilds the tree.

On a shared network drive, expect a few seconds' delay before a colleague's capture shows up. If it takes much longer than that, refresh — and see Sharing a library.
